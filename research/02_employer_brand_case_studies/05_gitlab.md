# GitLab: an operating handbook as candidate evidence

## Why this case

GitLab exposes an unusually large public Handbook alongside its candidate page. It lets this study examine whether operational documentation can serve candidate questions, and what happens when useful evidence is spread across a living document that needs search, maintenance, and context. [Candidate Handbook Page](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/candidate-faq/) · [Handbook Usage](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/about/handbook-usage/)

## Candidate decision context

A candidate may need to understand interview stages, whether a technical assessment applies, where work happens, and how a documented remote organization says it operates. GitLab's candidate page lists hiring stages, including assessments and technical interviews for certain roles. Its technical interview page describes a merge-request exercise for some positions. These are specific procedural inputs, though a candidate must still check the requirements for their own vacancy. [Candidate Handbook Page](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/candidate-faq/) · [Technical Interviews](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/interviewing/technical/)

## Observed behavior

The candidate page points applicants toward process and work information; the broader Handbook publishes operating guidance, including how GitLab says changes to the Handbook are proposed and communicated. GitLab's Handbook Direction page explicitly describes prospective applicants as an audience and frames the Handbook as a talent-acquisition resource. It also describes search, sharing, and freshness as design concerns. That direction page was rechecked on 2026-10-02 but is marked last modified **2025-09-02**; its roadmap and ownership statements are treated here as a dated published record, not verified current operating status. [Candidate Handbook Page](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/candidate-faq/) · [Handbook Usage](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/about/handbook-usage/) · [Handbook Direction](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/about/direction/)

## Evidence

The public evidence includes a candidate-facing process page, a role-conditional technical interview guide, and documentation of Handbook use. A candidate can inspect these directly. The dated Direction page explains GitLab's intended audience and acknowledges that navigability and freshness need work; it does not independently establish current ownership or progress on those concerns. [Candidate Handbook Page](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/candidate-faq/) · [Technical Interviews](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/interviewing/technical/) · [Handbook Direction](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/about/direction/)

## What this case supports

Operational documentation can disclose more about a stated work system than a careers page alone. It can let a candidate test a claim against a described process and prepare specific questions. The model depends on a trustworthy route to relevant pages, clear dates and ownership, and a way to distinguish a current rule from historical or aspirational text.

## What it does not prove

The Handbook does not establish that every team follows each documented process, that candidates can find the right page, or that reading it improves applicant outcomes. A large public corpus may be difficult to navigate, and page freshness varies. A published procedure is a claim about intended practice until checked against the role and current hiring team.

## Contribution to synthesis

GitLab shows that **organisational evidence** can be operational, while the **reality and governance check** includes document maintenance itself. Public availability is valuable only if a candidate can locate, date, interpret, and confirm the relevant guidance.
