# Canva: information across the application decision

## Why this case

Canva is a case about breadth and sequencing. Its careers site gives candidates distinct pages for hiring, early careers, career tools, growth, teams, and ways of working. The question is whether these inputs can be connected to the decisions a candidate makes before and during an application. [How We Hire](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/how-we-hire/) · [Early Careers](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/early-careers/) · [Career Tools](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/career-tools/)

## Candidate decision context

A candidate may need to decide whether a team and work style fit, whether they are eligible for an early-career route, and how to prepare for the hiring process. Canva's hiring page describes stages and notes variation by role and level; its engineering page identifies specialities such as product, platform, and data work. The benefits and ways-of-working page adds location and work-arrangement context. These questions are adjacent but not interchangeable. [How We Hire](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/how-we-hire/) · [Engineering](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/teams/engineering/) · [Benefits and Ways of Working](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/our-culture/benefits-ways-of-working/)

## Observed behavior

The hiring page presents a general process, interview preparation advice, accommodations, and a skills framework while distinguishing some engineering steps. Separate pages offer early-career routes and career tools for job seekers. The careers-and-growth page describes how Canva says it approaches feedback and development. Together these pages offer multiple possible entry points into a candidate decision, with a risk that a reader must assemble them across pages. ## Evidence

Canva's own pages provide process stages, stated skills categories, early-career information, team categories, and work-arrangement descriptions. The hiring page itself says the process varies by role, level, and specialty. That qualification matters: the general page cannot substitute for a specific vacancy or recruiter confirmation. [How We Hire](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/how-we-hire/) · [Engineering](https://www.lifeatcanva.com/en/teams/engineering/)

## What this case supports

Candidates can be offered different evidence at different decision moments: eligibility, work and team, process, preparation, and possible growth. A useful interface would route a candidate from their question to the relevant page, then to role-specific confirmation where needed. The observed public pages make such routing possible; this study did not test a candidate navigation task.

## What it does not prove

The pages do not show that candidates find, understand, or use the materials, that uncertainty falls, or that hiring outcomes improve. They do not provide a role-by-role mapping of skills to interview scores or demonstrate that every team implements the described growth and work practices alike. A broad information library can still leave a candidate with unanswered local questions.

## Contribution to synthesis

Canva highlights the **evidence interface**: having many accurate pages is insufficient if a candidate cannot connect them to a particular role and decision. Breadth creates a navigation and consistency obligation.
