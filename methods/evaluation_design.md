# Evaluation design: two distinct moments

## Early GEO exploration: qualitative only

Near the beginning of the inquiry, an exploratory prompt test asked whether relevant AI answers mentioned an organization, how they described it, and which information appeared to shape those descriptions. A contemporaneous public-information review examined fragmented or difficult-to-verify facts. The surviving test report is incomplete: its underlying source is missing, and exact conditions, source URLs, and screenshots were not retained. A separate small source-claim audit was another exploratory subset, not a substitute for the missing test source. These early materials support a qualitative information-quality question, not a clean baseline, a sample rate, or a causal finding. Their accumulation contributed to the earlier [Source-of-Truth reframing](../PROJECT_TIMELINE.md#early-inquiry-geo-and-information-quality).

## Later employee-story query baseline: a single recorded matrix

A different evaluation occurred after employee interviews, query-oriented content work, Golden Samples, a reusable writing process, and six story drafts. The six were **drafts**, with no evidence here of publication. This later baseline asked what AI answers surfaced, cited, omitted, or said they could not answer. It did not cause the earlier information-quality shift and cannot serve as a before-and-after test of the draft content.

The recorded design paired **12 prompts with five platforms**, producing **60 unique platform–prompt observations** and one completed answer for each cell. Five is the number of platforms. The model column uses several literal labels, but the number of distinct model families is not established. The first six prompts did not name the organization, yielding **30 eligible Discovery/Consideration outputs** for coding spontaneous mentions. The other six named it by design, yielding **30 prompted Brand/Due-diligence outputs**; their mentions are not spontaneous. The prompts and individual answers remain private.

### Three descriptive observations from that matrix

1. **Spontaneous mention:** **7 of 30** outputs in the unnamed-company Discovery/Consideration group mentioned the organization: six in Discovery and one contextual mention in Consideration. The latter was not a clear recommendation. This is a coded count within those eligible prompts, not a general visibility rate.
2. **Company-official source citation:** **13 of 60** outputs were coded as citing a company-official source. This was the protocol's binary citation code, with a URL recorded separately for positive rows. A citation does not prove that an answer's claim is accurate.
3. **Observable information limitation:** **40 of 60** outputs were manually coded as expressing at least one information limitation, such as inability to confirm a fact or insufficient public or employee information. Every positive row included an explanatory note. No inter-rater reliability record was found.

These are observations from **one mixed-condition matrix**, not population estimates, stable comparisons among models, or evidence that content changed answer behavior. Web search was enabled in 53 rows and disabled in seven. All rows were marked as clean/temporary sessions, but no screenshots were saved. Nineteen rows record reruns; the matrix retains completed outputs rather than all attempts. Each platform's first prompt had a punctuation difference from the frozen version. Conditions and literal model or tier labels varied, and one recorded answer per cell is not repeated independent trial evidence. There is no established post-publication lift or causal effect.
