# From manual cases to a research harness

## Why a protocol became useful

The first employer-brand cases were researched sequentially. Each required a candidate question, a source trail, a separation between company claims and inspectable evidence, and a boundary on what the case could show. The individual [case files](../research/02_employer_brand_case_studies/README.md) and [source register](../research/02_employer_brand_case_studies/sources.md) retain those distinctions.

As similar decisions recurred, the process was made explicit as a research protocol: define the case question; privilege public primary sources for company-specific facts; record the source and claim together; distinguish visible information from inferred candidate experience; challenge the strongest interpretation; and keep each case's limits attached to its synthesis. Consistent gates and verification made it possible to assign independent cases in parallel and compare them without flattening their differences. This describes the reasoning pattern, not the private implementation or its executable details.

## Observed process bounds

The reported estimates were approximately **one day** for the first case and **half a day each** for the second and third. Designing and building the harness took **most of a working day**. Two later case runs completed in parallel within roughly the same **one-hour-plus wall-clock window**. No independent elapsed-time record was found. The cases and build work are different activities, so these numbers are context-specific history, not a controlled timing comparison.

The harness made source policy, stage completion, and review more repeatable. It did not turn first-party careers pages into independent proof of employee experience or hiring outcomes. The [cross-case synthesis](../research/02_employer_brand_case_studies/cross_case_synthesis.md) remains a working interpretation of five selected cases, with transfer conditions and counterexamples still open.
