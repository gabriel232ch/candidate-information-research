# Candidate information research archive

**Question:** How can a candidate get relevant, checkable information about a role and its trade-offs before making a career decision? This archive follows an inquiry into reducing information asymmetry in that decision. It does not explain why people choose companies in general or establish which information changes hiring outcomes.

Gabriel Chen's inquiry page introduces an earlier question about smaller organizations. This repository holds the research trail and evidence boundaries behind the broader candidate-information inquiry.

## Current status and limits

This is an open research question. The comparisons are purposive, and much of the employer evidence is company-published. The archive shows information a reader could inspect and questions it raises; it does not verify day-to-day employee experience, representative candidate behavior, causal effects, or hiring and retention outcomes. Later content examples and an interactive concept are documented as process history, not as evidence of public launch or tested candidate benefit.

The archive contains public syntheses, method accounts, and source trails. Private interviews, employee material, raw prompt data, internal instructions, and implementation artifacts are outside its scope.
