# Project timeline: how the question changed

This is a chronology of research questions and reframings, not a claim that any one source proved a general theory. The [smaller-company study](research/01_smaller_high_impact_companies/README.md) and [five employer-brand cases](research/02_employer_brand_case_studies/README.md) contain the public evidence and their separate limits.

## Early inquiry: GEO and information quality

**Initial question — Would relevant AI answers surface an organization, describe it accurately, and show where their descriptions came from?** An exploratory prompt test examined mentions, descriptions, apparent source use, and facts that seemed fragmented or difficult to verify. Its surviving record is incomplete: the underlying test source is unavailable, and the detailed conditions and source links were not retained. It supports a qualitative observation about uneven recall and source traceability, not a numeric baseline. A separate review of the organization's public information also found ambiguity and dispersed facts. Together, those early signals moved the question upstream: what information would a person or answer system have available to draw on?

**Reframing — Source of Truth.** The work turned toward a structured, maintained account of basic organizational facts and claims, sometimes called a Brand Master in the working process. This was an information-quality response to the accumulation of early prompt exploration and public-information review. The private source material and its contents are outside this archive.

## External research: position and organizational reality

**Prior question — How should an organization explain its position?** The next inquiry asked whether smaller organizations can create meaningful output and attract essential talent, and which mechanisms make that possible. The [selected-company comparison](research/01_smaller_high_impact_companies/public_synthesis.md) shifted the emphasis from company size or brand language to position, usable leverage, ownership, visible work, and economic limits. Its selected cases generate hypotheses; they do not establish population rates or causes.

**New problem — Positioning could not substitute for organizational reality.** The comparison suggested that visible output, specialized leverage, ownership, and economic conditions mattered alongside how an organization described itself. That made the next question more concrete: what was the work actually like, and what could a prospective employee know about it? The selected cases inform this question; they do not prove a general relationship between employer messaging and talent outcomes.

## Employee evidence: from positioning to information gaps

**Prior question — Could employee stories demonstrate the proposed position?** In qualified aggregate terms, interviews indicated that some people had lacked useful information before joining about the real work, work style, role expectations, or internal environment. Individual accounts could not establish a typical employee experience, but they exposed a different use for employee content: evidence that could help future candidates answer questions about joining. No interview text or identifying detail is reproduced here.

## Candidate questions, content, and later evaluation

**New question — How could that evidence answer what a candidate asks?** The content unit moved from a general employee story to **candidate query → direct answer → relevant employee evidence → fuller story and context**. Three refined examples became Golden Samples for a reusable internal writing process; six candidate-query-oriented story drafts followed. The [content-method account](methods/golden_samples_to_reusable_system.md) describes the editorial judgments without reproducing the private examples or instructions. The six were drafts; the record does not establish publication.

**New problem — An answer needs an evaluation boundary.** A later structured evaluation asked what AI answers mentioned, cited, omitted, or said they could not establish. The [later single-matrix evaluation](methods/evaluation_design.md) contains three descriptive observations with their denominators and qualifications. It is chronologically and evidentially separate from the early GEO exploration, did not motivate the earlier Source-of-Truth shift, and cannot establish that the draft content changed answer behavior.

## From navigation to candidate decision context

**Intermediate question — How could candidates find the answers and supporting stories?** The first landing-page work organized discovery and navigation: entry points, groups of questions, and routes between direct answers and fuller accounts. This was an intermediate interface, with no established candidate-use or outcome evidence.

**New question — What makes organizational reality inspectable?** A second, planned program examined five independent employer-brand cases after that first landing-page stage. Unlike the earlier smaller-company comparison, it asked how public evidence about roles, work, growth, and fit might help candidates judge what joining would mean. Its [cross-case synthesis](research/02_employer_brand_case_studies/cross_case_synthesis.md) offers a bounded working interpretation: useful evidence may live in technical output, research, operating documents, and other artifacts, while freshness, access, and consistency still matter. Company-published pages cannot alone establish employee experience or candidate outcomes.

**Process question — How could the case method be reused?** Repeated source collection, claim checks, and case boundaries led to a [research harness](methods/manual_cases_to_research_harness.md) that made the protocol more consistent and allowed independent cases to run in parallel. Its reported timings are context-specific process history, not a measured efficiency gain.

**Current reframing — Support a candidate's decision, not merely an answer's mention.** The working synthesis led to an interactive crossroads concept organized around career decision contexts: competing paths, questions, evidence, and trade-offs. A prototype was completed and the direction selected for continued internal work. This does not establish public shipment, candidate validation, or completion of the latest internal iteration. The question remains open: what information helps a particular person make a better grounded career choice?
